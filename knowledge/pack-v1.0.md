## DOL PBM Final Rule: Fiduciary Action Checklist
What plan sponsors and their advisors should do now

### Your PBM Now Owes You Its Numbers. Your Job Is to Use Them.
On October 29, 2026, the Department of Labor released its final PBM fee disclosure rule. For plan years beginning on or after July 1, 2027, PBMs serving large self-insured ERISA plans must disclose, in dollars and drug by drug, what they and their affiliates earn: rebates kept, spread on every NDC, pharmacy clawbacks, termination fees, and the net price the plan pays. 
They must disclose it before you sign or renew, again every six months, and open their records to an auditor you choose.
The rule shifts the burden of proof. A PBM that fails to disclose is in a prohibited transaction. A fiduciary who receives the disclosures and does nothing with them has a documentation problem of its own. Every dollar retained upstream is a dollar your employees and their families pay through premiums and cost sharing.
| Date | What happens |
| February 3, 2026 | Consolidated Appropriations Act (CAA) of 2026 disclosure duty under ERISA 408(b)(2)(B) took effect. DOL grants good-faith enforcement relief until the rule applies |
| 60 days after Federal Register publication | Final rule becomes effective |
| 90 days before your first renewal on or after July 1, 2027 | Initial disclosures due from your PBM (October 2027 for a January 1, 2028 renewal) |
| Plan years beginning on or after July 1, 2027 | Rule applies. For calendar-year plans: January 1, 2028 |
| Plan years beginning on or after August 3, 2028 | CAA 2026 100% rebate pass-through and new semiannual PBM reports apply |

Items marked pending counsel confirmation rest on our reading and await legal review.

### Step 0: Know Which Rules Reach Your Plan
The answer turns on three facts: whether ERISA governs the plan, whether it's self-insured, and whether it has 100 or more employees or participants. Level-funded plans count as self-insured. Self-insured dental and vision plans are covered plans too, so check whether any of their vendors provide pharmacy-related services.
| Plan type | DOL final rule | CAA 2026 pass-through (2028) | CAA 2026 PBM reports (2028) |
| ERISA, self-insured or level-funded, 100+ | Applies in full | Applies | Detailed semiannual report |
| ERISA, self-insured, under 100 | General statutory disclosures only | Applies | Summary report |
| ERISA, fully insured | Detailed rule doesn't apply. General statutory disclosures still required | Applies through the insurer | Summary report; large plans may opt in to detailed |
| Non-federal governmental (county, city, school district) | Does not apply | Does not apply | Applies |
| Church plan (non-electing) | Does not apply | Does not apply | Applies |

Two counting rules matter. Single-employer plans count employees using the ACA employer aggregation rules. Multiemployer plans and MEWAs count participants, averaged over the prior plan year.
Governmental and church plans still carry fiduciary duties under state law and plan documents. For them, every item below becomes a contract ask: require the PBM to make these disclosures and accept these audit terms as if ERISA applied.
- Confirm ERISA status, funding arrangement, and headcount for the current year
- Identify who signed the pharmacy contract. If a TPA, carrier, or level-funded arranger contracts for pharmacy services, that entity owes you the disclosures, even when another company runs the PBM
- Ask your broker or consultant in writing whether it is affiliated with your PBM. Affiliated brokers and consultants must disclose under the rule

### Now Through Mid-2027: Know Your Contract Before Your PBM Explains It
The grace period is a head start, not a pause. DOL confirmed the statutory disclosure duty has applied since February 3, 2026, and it protects fiduciaries who act in good faith until the rule applies. Good faith leaves a paper trail.
- Pull the complete contract. The master agreement, every pricing and rebate exhibit, every amendment, performance guarantees, the business associate agreement, and the renewal, notice, and termination terms. Most sponsors have never assembled the full set in one place. If your PBM or TPA holds the only copy, request it in writing.
- Run a Contract X-Ray analysis. It evaluates the contract against Fiduciary-Aligned PBM Contract Standards organized into 10 categories, from rebate pass-through and audit rights to data ownership and clean exit, and produces a Negotiation Guide: the specific terms to request, the model language to propose, and the fiduciary rationale for each. That report becomes your renewal agenda and part of your documented process.
- Request statutory disclosures now from your PBM, TPA, broker, and any other provider paid $1,000 or more. ERISA 408(b)(2)(B) has required them since February 3, 2026. Keep the request and the response. For your TPA, ask for the items DOL lists: administrative and per-employee-per-month fees, 340B-related income, provider and network compensation, affiliate and vertically integrated compensation, data monetization, and payment and recovery compensation.
- Document a reasonableness review of the current arrangement, using the disclosures and the Contract X-Ray results. Minutes, not memory.
- Build a renewal calendar. List every pharmacy-related contract, its renewal date, and its auto-renewal notice window. Work backward: the Negotiation Guide should be in hand before the PBM's 90-day disclosure window opens.
- Name the responsible plan fiduciary. The rule sends disclosures to the fiduciary with authority to sign, extend, or renew. Confirm who that is, and update the committee charter so disclosure review and audit decisions have an owner.
- Complete contract assembled and stored with the plan's fiduciary records
- Contract X-Ray analysis and Negotiation Guide received
- Written disclosure requests sent to PBM, TPA, and broker
- Reasonableness review documented in committee minutes
- Renewal calendar built, with auto-renewal notice dates
- Responsible plan fiduciary named; committee charter updated
- Multi-year contract running past July 1, 2027 without a renewal: ask counsel how the rule applies (pending counsel confirmation)

### Before Your Next Renewal: Negotiate the Terms the Rule Leaves Open
The rule sets a floor and leaves several dials to you. Set them in the contract, because whatever you don't elect defaults to the PBM's preference.
Terms to elect. The rule permits each of these; none happens unless you ask.
| Ask | Default if you say nothing | Source |
| Quarterly disclosure reports | Semiannual | 2550.408b-22(e)(2), (g) |
| Materiality threshold below 5% for formulary-change notices | 5% of manufacturer payments | (e)(6)(iii) |
| Overage explanation threshold below 5% | 5% over estimate | (g)(4) |
| Machine-readable disclosure files within 30 days of request | "Reasonable time," undefined | (k)(3) |
| Audit "year" defined as the plan year | Left to the parties | (j)(1) |
| Initial disclosures more than 90 days before renewal | 90 days | (e) |

Terms to strike. For covered plans, each of these makes the arrangement unreasonable under ERISA 408(b)(2) once the rule applies. Contract X-Ray flags them in the Negotiation Guide.
| Contract term | Why it fails | Source |
| PBM approval of, or limits on, your choice of auditor | Plan selects the auditor | (j)(2) |
| Audits less than once a year | At least annual, at plan request | (j)(1) |
| Limits on audit period, location, or number of records | No PBM restrictions | (j)(6) |
| Upstream rebate and aggregator contracts walled off from audit | PBM must produce them, plus affiliate records, under reasonable confidentiality | (j)(3), (j)(7) |
| Fees charged to the plan for producing audit data | PBM bears the cost of providing information | (j)(4) |
| Audit data due later than 60 days after request | 60-day outer limit | (j)(5) |
| Confidentiality clauses barring you from sharing the contract or disclosures with consultants, auditors, or competing bidders | PBM may only require reasonable confidentiality on redisclosure; regulators and counsel always allowed | (k)(4) |
| No exit without penalty on reasonably short notice | Preamble restates existing DOL rule (pending counsel confirmation) | 29 CFR 2550.408b-2(c)(3) |
| Mandatory statistical sampling in audits | DOL declined to permit sampling expressly (pending counsel confirmation) | (j)(6) |

Terms prudence calls for. The rule is silent on these. Ask anyway.
- No-fee termination right if the PBM fails to cure a disclosure or audit failure within 90 days
- All rebates earned through the termination date paid in full
- Plan data returned in usable form within 30 days of termination, at no charge
- PBM indemnifies the plan for losses caused by inaccurate disclosures
- PBM represents in the contract that every required disclosure is complete and accurate
- Audit rights cover contract compliance and permit extrapolation, beyond verifying the disclosures
- A "higher of" clause: a broader regulatory definition of remuneration or audit scope applies automatically; a narrower one leaves the contract intact
- Every RFP response discloses the bidder's full economics in the rule's disclosure categories, so competing offers compare line by line

### When Disclosures Arrive: Read Them, Test Them, Act on Them
Disclosure is the raw material. The fiduciary act is what you do next, and DOL expects to see it written down.
Review each initial disclosure against the contract and your Contract X-Ray results. A contract promising 100% pass-through next to a disclosure showing retained manufacturer payments is a finding, not a footnote.
- Disclosure arrived at least 90 days before renewal, or by the date your contract sets
- Every required element is present: services; direct compensation by service; manufacturer payments per drug, passed through and retained; spread per NDC and by channel; affiliated pharmacy steering; pharmacy clawbacks; price protection; termination compensation; other compensation and who pays it; formulary incentives and omitted lower-cost alternatives; net price per fill; fiduciary status; audit rights
- Amounts stated in dollars, with estimates tied to a stated method
- Retained amounts reconciled against the contract's pass-through and fee terms
- Drugs with retained manufacturer payments checked against the therapeutically equivalent alternatives the PBM left off the formulary
- Revenue the PBM or its partners earn from copay maximizer, accumulator, or alternative funding programs identified and explained
- Reasonableness determination recorded in committee minutes before signing
Track the semiannual reports. Each is due within 30 days after the period closes. Any category running 5% or more over the estimate, or your lower threshold, requires a written explanation of the overage.
Decide on an audit every year, and record the decision. The plan pays the auditor and the PBM pays to produce the data. DOL says an audit isn't required absent red flags, but the choice to skip one must be a prudent, documented choice. Budget for it, choose an independent auditor, and send the request in writing. The PBM must acknowledge within 10 business days and produce within 60 calendar days.
Follow the relief protocol the moment you find a failure. A missing disclosure or a stonewalled audit is the PBM's prohibited transaction. It becomes yours too unless you act on these steps. Relief is available when you did not know of the failure and reasonably believed the disclosures were made.
- Send the PBM a written request to correct the failure as soon as you discover it.
- If the PBM cures within 90 calendar days, relief is preserved. Keep monitoring.
- If it refuses or misses the 90 days, notify DOL within 30 days of the refusal or of day 90, whichever comes first.
- Decide whether to terminate or continue the contract under your duty of prudence, and document the basis.
The decision record should name the alternatives you weighed and how you'll prevent a repeat. DOL says it should not be a check-the-box exercise. Keep it with the plan's fiduciary records. (2550.408b-22(n))

### August 2028: Sign 2027 Contracts for the Law Arriving Next
When the 2028 requirements reach your contract. Two things have to be true: your contract is signed, renewed, or extended, and it covers a plan year that starts on or after August 3, 2028. For a calendar-year plan, the first such plan year starts January 1, 2029. Your contract's end date doesn't matter on its own. What matters is whether the contract covers a plan year that starts on or after August 3, 2028.
| Scenario (calendar-year plan unless noted) | Do the 2028 requirements apply during this contract? |
| You sign tomorrow. The contract ends August 4, 2028. | No. Your 2028 plan year started January 1, 2028, before the cutoff. The requirements arrive with your next contract or renewal. |
| You sign tomorrow. The contract runs through December 31, 2028. | No. Same reason: it never covers a plan year starting after August 3, 2028. |
| You sign in 2027 for three years, through December 31, 2029. | Unsettled. The contract covers the 2029 plan year, which starts after the cutoff. One reading says the requirements apply for 2029; another says they wait for your next renewal. Pending counsel confirmation. Write them into the contract for 2029 now. |
| The contract auto-renews on January 1, 2029. | Yes, under the better reading. An automatic renewal for the 2029 plan year is a renewal. Some practitioners treat evergreen terms differently, so put the answer in writing. |
| You amend pricing mid-term in 2029. | Unsettled. The statute doesn't say whether an amendment counts as an extension. Treat any amendment as a renewal in the contract language. |
| Your plan year starts July 1. | First covered plan year: July 1, 2029. July 1, 2028 is before the cutoff. |
| Your plan year starts September 1. | First covered plan year: September 1, 2028. |
| You're a county, city, school district, or church plan. | The pass-through does not apply by statute; it amends ERISA only. The reporting requirements do apply. Contract for the pass-through anyway. |

The practical answer: any contract you sign from now on that runs past mid-2028 should require the PBM to comply with the CAA 2026 PBM requirements starting with your first plan year that begins on or after August 3, 2028, regardless of contract cycle. It should also treat any renewal, extension, auto-renewal, or amendment as a renewal. Then the open questions don't matter, because the contract has already answered them.
The DOL disclosure rule runs on its own clock: plan years beginning on or after July 1, 2027, or January 1, 2028 for calendar-year plans.
What the statute requires of PBMs serving ERISA plans:
- Remit 100% of rebates, fees, alternative discounts, and other remuneration from manufacturers, rebate aggregators, and affiliated GPOs, quarterly, within 90 days of quarter end (ERISA 408(b)(2)(C))
- Rebate aggregators and PBM-affiliated GPOs must remit to the PBM within 45 days of quarter end
- Return underpayments within 90 days of notice
- Open remittance records and rebate contracts to audit at least once per plan year, by an auditor the plan selects and the PBM does not pay
- Keep "bona fide service fees" only if they are transparent and quantifiable. DOL has not yet defined the term
- These duties fall on whoever provides pharmacy benefit services, including carriers, TPAs, and rebate aggregators, whatever they call themselves
What the statute requires for all group health plans, governmental and church plans included (ERISA 726, PHSA 2799A-11, IRC 9826):
- Semiannual PBM reports, quarterly on request, in plain language and machine-readable format. Plans with 100 or more get drug-level and therapeutic-class detail, including spread on every claim
- Disclosure of what the PBM pays brokers and consultants to win or keep your business, with the recipient named
- An annual notice from the plan to participants about the PBM reporting requirement
- Civil penalties for PBMs and others that fail to report: up to $10,000 a day, and up to $100,000 for each item of knowingly false information
Spread pricing is reported under the statute, not banned. If you want it gone, the contract has to say so.
- Require compliance with the 2028 provisions by a fixed date, the first plan year beginning on or after August 3, 2028, regardless of contract cycle
- Define any amendment, extension, or auto-renewal as a renewal for CAA 2026 purposes
- Define remuneration broadly: any value, in any form, from any manufacturer, aggregator, or affiliate, including margin retained between plan payment and pharmacy reimbursement
- Require pass-through pricing so no spread exists
- Define a manufacturer fee as bona fide only if it is flat, fair market value, for an identified service, unrelated to price or volume, and itemized by payer
- Governmental and church plans: require the PBM to meet the 408(b)(2)(C) pass-through terms as if ERISA applied
The 2028 items rest on the enacted text of CAA 2026 Sections 6701 and 6702 and await counsel confirmation.

§2550.408b-22 Compensation transparency; pharmacy benefit management services.

(a) General. Section 408(b)(2) of the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (the Act) exempts from the prohibitions of section 406(a) of the Act payment by a plan to a party

[Final rule, p. 331]

in interest, including a fiduciary, for office space or any service (or a combination of services) if such office space or service is furnished under a contract or arrangement which is reasonable. No contract or arrangement for services between a covered plan and a covered service provider, nor any extension or renewal, is reasonable within the meaning of section 408(b)(2) of the Act unless, in addition to meeting the general requirements in § 2550.408b-2, the requirements of section 408(b)(2)(B) of the Act are met. This regulation implements ERISA section 408(b)(2)(B) with respect to the covered service providers defined herein. A contract or arrangement between a covered plan and a covered service provider as defined herein that satisfies the requirements of this regulation also satisfies the requirements of section 408(b)(2)(B) of the Act.

(b) Covered plan. For purposes of this section, a “covered plan” means a group health plan as defined in section 733(a) of the Act that is offered by a specified large employer or that is a specified large plan, each as defined in paragraph (m), other than (1) a group health plan in which all of the benefits are provided exclusively through a contract or policy of insurance issued by a health insurance issuer as defined in § 2590.701-2 of this chapter and (2) a group health plan established by a plan sponsor that is, or that is affiliated with, a drug manufacturer, distributor, wholesaler, pharmacy benefit manager, rebate aggregator (or other purchasing entity designed to aggregate rebates).

(c) Covered service provider. (1) For purposes of this section, a “covered service provider” means a service provider that enters a contract or arrangement with the covered plan and reasonably expects $1,000 or more in compensation, direct or indirect, to be received in connection with:

(i) Providing any pharmacy benefit management services, as defined in paragraph (d) of this section, pursuant to the service contract or arrangement, regardless of whether such services

[Final rule, p. 332]

will be performed, or such compensation received, by the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor; or

(ii) Providing advice, recommendations, or referrals regarding the provision of pharmacy benefit management services, as defined in paragraph (d) of this section, pursuant to the service contract or arrangement, and is the entity described in paragraph (c)(1)(i) of this section or an affiliate of such entity.

(2) No person or entity is a “covered service provider” solely on the basis of providing services as an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor of the covered service provider, with respect to performing one or more of the services described in paragraph (c)(1)(i) or (ii) of this section under the contract or arrangement with the covered plan.

(3) Nothing in this section requires disclosure of the compensation of an individual who is an officer, director, employee, or partner of a covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent or a subcontractor, received for their duties performed as set forth in their job description or as expected in their role in connection with the provision of pharmacy benefit management services described in paragraph (d).

(d) Pharmacy benefit management services—

(1) General. For purposes of this section, the term “pharmacy benefit management services” means services necessary for the management or administration of a covered plan’s prescription drug benefits (including the covered plan’s provision of prescription drugs through the plan’s medical benefit), regardless of whether the person, business, or entity performing the service identifies itself as a “pharmacy benefit manager.”

(2) Examples. Pharmacy benefit management services include but are not limited to:

[Final rule, p. 333]

(i) Acting as a negotiator or aggregator of rebates, fees, discounts, and other price concessions for prescription drugs.

(ii) Establishing or maintaining prescription drug formularies.

(iii) Establishing or maintaining pharmacy networks, through contract or otherwise, including a mail order pharmacy, a specialty pharmacy, a retail pharmacy, a nursing home pharmacy, a long-term care pharmacy, and an infusion or other outpatient pharmacy, to provide prescription drugs.

(iv) Performing utilization review and management, including the instituting or processing of prior authorization requests for drugs, step therapy protocols, patient compliance analyses, conducting therapeutic intervention, and administering generic and biosimilar substitution programs.

(v) In conjunction with any of these other services, processing and payment of claims for prescription drugs; adjudicating appeals or grievances related to the covered plan’s prescription drug benefits; recordkeeping related to the covered plan’s prescription drug benefits; or performing regulatory compliance with respect to the covered plan’s prescription drug benefits under the service contract or arrangement.

(e) Initial disclosure requirements. A covered service provider shall disclose to a responsible plan fiduciary, in writing, the following information in paragraphs (e)(1) through (9) of this section, not later than the date that is reasonably in advance of the date on which the service contract or arrangement is entered, and extended or renewed (for extensions and renewals, 90 calendar days in advance is deemed to be a reasonable period of time):

(1) Description of services. A description of each pharmacy benefit management service, or of the advice, recommendations, or referrals regarding the provision of pharmacy benefit

[Final rule, p. 334]

management services, to be provided to the covered plan pursuant to the service contract or arrangement.

(2) Direct compensation. A description of the amount of all direct compensation, both in the aggregate and by service, that the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor reasonably expects to receive on a semiannual (or at the request of the covered plan, quarterly) basis in connection with services under the service contract or arrangement. For purposes of this paragraph (e)(2), the term “direct compensation” means compensation received directly from a covered plan or from the plan sponsor on behalf of the plan (regardless of whether such compensation is paid from plan assets). An example is an administrative fee calculated on a per-participant, per-month basis. In the case of a bundled service or aggregated compensation arrangement, the description must include a reasonable and good faith allocation of the compensation associated with each service in the bundle.

(3) Indirect compensation. A description of the following, which shall include compensation based on a structure of incentives not solely related to the contract or arrangement with the covered plan:

(i) Manufacturer payments. A description of the amount of any rebate, fee, alternative discount or other remuneration, both in the aggregate and for each drug on the formulary, reasonably expected to be received from any drug manufacturer, distributor, wholesaler, or rebate aggregator (or other purchasing entity designed to aggregate rebates), by the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor in connection with the service contract or arrangement, for the semiannual period (or quarter) (A) for claims incurred during the period and (B) related to utilization of such drug or spending on such drug during the period, specifying both the amount that is reasonably expected to be passed on to the covered plan and, if

[Final rule, p. 335]

applicable, plan sponsor and the amount that will be retained by the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor.

(ii) Spread compensation.

(A) A list of drugs on the formulary and with respect to each such drug on such list:

(1) the contracted compensation to be paid by the covered plan for each drug (identified by the National Drug Code) to the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor on behalf of the covered plan;

(2) the contracted compensation to be paid to the pharmacy by the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor on behalf of the covered plan for each drug (identified by the National Drug Code);

(3) for each drug, the difference between the amount to be paid under subparagraph (1) and the amount paid under subparagraph (2) (spread compensation);

(B) A description of the amount of spread compensation reasonably expected to be received by the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor in connection with the service contract or arrangement, for claims filed during each semiannual period (or quarter) both in the aggregate and for each drug on the formulary, and for each pharmacy channel (i.e., retail, mail order, and specialty pharmacy); and

(C) A description of arrangements, incentives, contractual provisions, and policies that will be implemented under the service contract or arrangement that direct patients to pharmacies that are affiliates of the covered service provider.

(iii) Pharmacy Recoupment. A description of the amount of copay claw-back compensation and any other retroactive adjustment to pharmacy reimbursement reasonably expected to be recouped from a pharmacy by a covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or

[Final rule, p. 336]

a subcontractor in connection with prescription drugs dispensed under the service contract or arrangement for each semiannual period (or quarter), specifying the anticipated total number of transactions resulting in recoupment. For purpose of this paragraph (e)(3)(iii), copay claw-back compensation means the dollar amount of the difference between a copayment or coinsurance amount paid to the pharmacy by a plan participant or beneficiary and the reimbursement to the pharmacy.

(iv) Price protection agreements. A description of any inflation protection or price protection agreements that the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor has entered with any drug manufacturer, distributor, wholesaler, or rebate aggregator (or other purchasing entity designed to aggregate rebates), in connection with prescription drugs dispensed under the service contract or arrangement, specifying the amount reasonably expected to be retained by the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor in connection with each such inflation protection or price protection contract or arrangement for each semiannual period (or quarter) (A) for claims incurred during the period and (B) that is related to utilization of such drug or spending on such drug during the period, and the amount that will be passed on to the plan and, if applicable, plan sponsor.

(4) Compensation for termination of service contract or arrangement. A description of any compensation that the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor reasonably expects to receive in connection with termination of the service contract or arrangement, and how any prepaid amounts will be calculated and refunded upon such termination.

(5) Description of other compensation. To the extent not already disclosed under paragraphs (e)(1) through (4) of this section—

[Final rule, p. 337]

(i) A description of any compensation that the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor reasonably expects to receive in connection with the service contract or arrangement for the semiannual period (or quarter), regardless of whether such compensation is paid regularly or irregularly or from plan assets or is subject to any contingency or other condition;

(ii) The identification of the payer of such compensation;

(iii) An identification of the services for which such compensation will be received; and

(iv) A description of the arrangement between the payer and the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor, as applicable, pursuant to which such compensation is paid.

(6) Description of formulary placement incentives.

(i) A description of any formulary placement incentives and arrangements that the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor has entered with any drug manufacturer in connection with the service contract or arrangement, along with an explanation of how the incentives and arrangements affect services to and are aligned with the interests of the plan and/or its participants and beneficiaries (e.g., incentives or arrangements are to control prescription drug costs, provide clinically superior drugs, or both).

(ii) For any drug on the formulary with respect to which the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor reasonably expects to receive any payment by the manufacturer or aggregator in connection with the service contract or arrangement (and that is not passed through to the plan), an identification of any reasonably available therapeutically equivalent alternatives (including biosimilar biological products), and the reason for omitting the alternatives from the formulary.

[Final rule, p. 338]

(iii) If the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor retains authority to modify the formulary during the term of the service contract or arrangement, such as by adding or deleting drugs or changing their tiering, an explanation of the reasons for retaining such authority, the expected frequency of such changes, and that the responsible plan fiduciary will be notified reasonably in advance of any modifications that, individually or in the aggregate, are reasonably expected to have a material impact on the reasonableness of compensation under the service contract or arrangement, as well as the covered plan’s right to terminate the service contract or arrangement on reasonably short notice under the circumstances. For purposes of this paragraph (e)(6)(iii), the term “material” means an amount that is 5 percent or more, or such lower percentage or dollar amount as may be agreed to by the responsible plan fiduciary and set forth in writing in the contract or arrangement, of the aggregate compensation (on a semiannual basis, or at the request of the covered plan, quarterly basis) disclosed pursuant to paragraph (e)(3)(i) of this section, adjusted for any increases previously disclosed under this paragraph (e)(6).

(7) Drug pricing methodology. A description of the net price per course of treatment or single fill, such as a 30-day supply or 90-day supply, to the covered plan of each drug on the formulary, as defined by National Drug Code, after rebates, fees, alternative discounts, or other remuneration received from drug manufacturers, distributors, wholesalers, or rebate aggregators (or other purchasing entities designed to aggregate rebates), for each pharmacy channel, expressed as a monetary amount. If a monetary amount is not ascertainable, the covered service provider must disclose the methodology used by the covered service provider, an affiliate, an agent, or a subcontractor, under the service contract or arrangement, to determine the net price

[Final rule, p. 339]

per course of treatment or single fill, the covered plan will pay for each drug on the formulary, for each pharmacy channel, along with an objective means to verify the accuracy.

11.3. Claims and Transaction-level Data

Many commenters asked the Department to require claims data and other transaction- level data be provided to responsible plan fiduciaries. Commenters argued that claims and transactions-level data originate from the group health plan and its participants and beneficiaries and therefore should be treated as a plan asset. Some of these comments asked the Department to confirm that such information would be available under the audit provision, which the Department has addressed in section E.8. of this preamble. The Department confirms that such information must be made available under the audit provision to the extent required to verify any, or all, of the disclosures required under this rule and has added that language to the regulatory text. However, the Department declines to mandate the affirmative disclosure of such data in this final rule outside of the context of an audit to verify disclosures, to keep the final rule focused on covered service provider, affiliate, agent, and subcontractor services, compensation and compensation arrangements.

11.5. Standardized Template

Commenters also asked the Department to create a standardized data template to assist covered service providers as well as responsible plan fiduciaries. 11.6. Guidance and Interpretive Questions

Commenters asked the Department to provide guidance for responsible plan fiduciaries regarding how to evaluate the disclosures provided under the final rule. For example, several commenters asked the Department to establish a process-based safe harbor under ERISA section 404(a) for fiduciaries who follow a structured analytics process when selecting a covered service provider to a group health plan. One commenter outlined numerous benefits with a process-based safe harbor, including upfront due diligence in contract negotiation and vendor selection, reduced compliance burdens, enhanced participant protection, and statutory alignment that would create a unified compliance framework for the responsible plan fiduciary. The Department strongly agrees and is considering separate rulemaking under ERISA section 404(a) for group health plans.
